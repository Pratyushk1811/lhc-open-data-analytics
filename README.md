# CERN CMS Open Data: Dimuon Invariant Mass Spectrum Pipeline

A high-performance data pipeline written in Python to reconstruct fundamental subatomic particles from raw collision data generated by the CMS Detector at the Large Hadron Collider (CERN).

## 🚀 Project Overview
This project processes a dataset of 100,000 real proton-proton collision events from CERN Open Data. When heavy, unstable particles are created in these high-energy collisions, they decay instantly into pairs of muons ($\mu\mu$). 

By extracting the 3D momentum vectors and total energies of these child particles, this pipeline applies relativistic mechanics backwards to calculate the **Invariant Mass** of the parent particles, successfully revealing quantum states hidden within the raw data noise.

## 🧮 The Physics & Math
Individual particle energy and momentum change depending on the angle of decay. However, **Invariant Mass ($M$)** is an absolute constant. The pipeline solves the relativistic energy-momentum tensor equation for every event simultaneously:

$$M = \sqrt{(E_1 + E_2)^2 - (p_{x1} + p_{x2})^2 - (p_{y1} + p_{y2})^2 - (p_{z1} + p_{z2})^2}$$

## 📊 What the Data Reveals
When plotting the calculated invariant masses on a logarithmic scale, distinct statistical spikes pierce through the continuous combinatorial background "fog":

1. **The $J/\psi$ Meson ($\approx 3.1$ GeV):** Proves the existence of the Charm Quark. It shows up as an incredibly sharp, towering needle on the left side of the spectrum.
2. **The Upsilon Family ($\approx 9.5$ GeV):** Reveals the Bottom Quark. In this high-resolution plot, this peak beautifully splits into sub-peaks representing the different internal quantum vibrational states of the particle ($\Upsilon(1S), \Upsilon(2S), \Upsilon(3S)$).
3. **The $Z$ Boson ($\approx 91$ GeV):** The heavy fundamental force carrier of the Weak Nuclear Force. Its broader, dome-like peak shape visually demonstrates Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle ($\Delta E \Delta t \approx \hbar$) due to its incredibly short lifetime.
